We develop computational methods and software for quantitative analysis of scientific imaging data. With collaborators in biology and medicine, we turn volumetric and time-series microscopy data into reproducible, interpretable results—from cellular dynamics to tissue-scale organization.

Our research spans:

- spatiotemporal analysis of cellular and developmental microscopy datasets, using hierarchical and graph-based representations to characterize objects, relationships, and dynamics across space and time;
- machine- and deep-learning approaches for image segmentation, tracking, feature extraction, and phenotypic classification;
- analysis of fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy (FLIM) and related multi-parameter assays of cellular metabolic and redox state;
- portable, modular analysis pipelines built on workflow orchestration and containerization that support reproducible science and scale from local workstations to HPC and cloud environments.
